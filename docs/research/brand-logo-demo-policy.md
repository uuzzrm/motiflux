# Third-party logo demonstrations in Motiflux

> Research note — 2026-08-18. This is a conservative risk assessment, not legal advice. It uses official first-party sources only. No third-party logo files were downloaded or added to the repository.

## Decision

Do not add real third-party company logos to Motiflux’s animated showcase, even while the GitHub repository is private, unless the relevant owner has granted written permission or a specific published license clearly covers the exact use.

A private repository is lower-exposure than a public repository, but it is not an intellectual-property safe harbor. It does not grant permission from the logo owner, and it does not remove the need to comply with GitHub’s policies. Animation is an additional risk because it can alter or transform the mark and can make the logo a prominent visual subject rather than a necessary factual reference.

For Motiflux, the recommended implementation is:

- use the supplied Prysai logo and fictional, self-created industry stand-ins for the 13 animation demonstrations;
- use company names as plain text only when a factual reference is genuinely useful;
- label any internal experiment `unofficial design study — not sponsored, endorsed, or affiliated`;
- keep any real-mark experiment private, access-limited, noncommercial, and out of README images, GitHub Pages, PDFs intended for distribution, screenshots, and public demos;
- before public or client-facing publication, remove real marks unless written permission has been obtained.

This recommendation preserves the design-learning goal while avoiding a claim that a private GitHub repository makes third-party logo animation permitted.

## Risk distinctions

### Copyright, trademark, and permission are different questions

- **Copyright:** asks whether the logo artwork or source image is a protected creative work and whether copying or modifying it is authorized or otherwise defensible.
- **Trademark:** asks whether the presentation creates confusion about source, affiliation, sponsorship, endorsement, or approval.
- **Permission:** asks whether the owner’s own terms or a written license authorizes the exact activity. A repository being private is not permission.

### Private development versus public publication

Private development generally lowers the audience, distribution, and marketing risk. It does not eliminate owner rights, GitHub policy obligations, or the possibility of access under GitHub’s stated exceptions. Public README images, Pages, PDFs, social posts, portfolio pages, and client presentations increase exposure and can make a design demonstration look promotional or endorsement-like.

### Nominative reference versus endorsement

The safer reference pattern is a small, plain-text statement that identifies the subject or a truthful compatibility relationship. The riskier pattern is a prominent logo treated as the visual hero, especially when it is altered, recolored, combined with Motiflux/Prysai branding, shown beside claims of partnership, or animated as if the owner supplied or approved the result. A disclaimer can reduce confusion, but it is not a substitute for permission where the owner’s guidelines require permission.

## Conservative Motiflux policy

1. **Default:** real third-party logos are not showcase inputs.
2. **Preferred substitute:** create original, fictional marks that express the industry category without copying a real company’s logo, wordmark, color system, mascot, or distinctive trade dress.
3. **Text-only references:** permit only factual, necessary references; use the company name as text and avoid logo files.
4. **Internal exception:** if a rights holder has given written permission, record the permission scope, asset version, allowed channels, modification/animation rights, attribution requirements, and expiry/revocation terms outside the public artifact. Keep the repository private and access-limited until verified.
5. **No implied relationship:** do not call any company a customer, partner, benchmark participant, integration, or sponsor unless that relationship is real and documented.
6. **Public-release gate:** before making the repository public or publishing the showcase, replace real marks with fictional stand-ins unless the exact permission is documented.

Suggested internal notice:

> Unofficial design study. Referenced names are used only to identify the subject of the study. This prototype is not sponsored, endorsed, approved, or affiliated with any referenced company. All marks remain the property of their respective owners.

The notice is a transparency measure, not a legal clearance.

## Scope and limitations

This note answers the narrow question of whether a private GitHub repository makes third-party logo animation safe. It does not determine fair use in a particular jurisdiction, validate ownership of a particular logo file, or replace advice from qualified counsel. Nike’s cited source is an official FAQ rather than a full public brand-animation guideline; no Nike-specific permission is inferred from the absence of a more detailed public guide.
